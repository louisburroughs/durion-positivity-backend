# Tire & Service Shop Management Guide
*A practical operating guide for front counter, service advisors, and shop managers*

---

## 1. Overview

This guide defines the standard operating procedures for running a modern tire and service shop. It focuses on:

- Customer intake and appointment scheduling
- Vehicle check-in and work order creation
- Estimate preparation and approval workflows
- Communication and service execution
- Checkout and follow-up

The objective is consistency, transparency, and efficiency while maintaining a high level of customer trust.

---

## 2. Roles & Responsibilities

### Service Advisor (Front Counter)
- Primary customer contact
- Schedules appointments
- Creates work orders and estimates
- Secures customer approvals
- Communicates status updates

### Technician
- Performs inspections and service
- Documents findings
- Recommends additional work

### Shop Manager
- Oversees workflow and scheduling
- Handles escalations
- Ensures quality and profitability

---

## 3. Appointment Scheduling

### 3.1 Intake Channels
Appointments may originate from:
- Phone calls
- Walk-ins
- Online booking system
- Fleet/customer accounts

### 3.2 Required Information
Capture the following:

- Customer name
- Phone number and/or email
- Vehicle:
  - Year, Make, Model
  - License plate (if available)
  - Mileage (estimate acceptable)
- Requested service (e.g., tires, oil change, brake inspection)
- Preferred date/time

### 3.3 Scheduling Process

1. **Check availability**
   - Review technician capacity and bay availability
   - Consider service duration

2. **Assign appointment slot**
   - Match service type to technician skillset if required
   - Avoid overbooking

3. **Set expectations**
   - Provide estimated service duration
   - Clarify drop-off vs wait appointment
   - Communicate any preconditions (e.g., arrive 10 minutes early)

4. **Confirm appointment**
   - Provide confirmation verbally or via SMS/email
   - Include:
     - Date/time
     - Services requested
     - Shop contact info

---

## 4. Customer Check-In (Vehicle Intake)

### 4.1 Greeting and Verification

1. Greet customer promptly
2. Confirm:
   - Name
   - Appointment details (if scheduled)
   - Vehicle identity

3. For walk-ins:
   - Assess workload and fit into schedule
   - Provide realistic wait time

---

### 4.2 Vehicle Walkaround (Recommended Best Practice)

Perform a quick inspection with the customer:

- Existing damage (scratches, dents)
- Tire condition
- Dashboard warnings
- Notable issues

Document findings to avoid disputes.

---

### 4.3 Create Work Order

Enter:

- Customer details (verify or create record)
- Vehicle details (VIN if available)
- Mileage (capture accurately if possible)
- Complaint / Requested services (customer-stated)
- Advisor notes

**Best Practice:**
Use clear, structured language:
- “Customer states vibration at highway speeds”
- “Customer requests 4 tire replacement”

---

### 4.4 Assign Priority & Type

- Waiter / Drop-off
- Scheduled / Emergency
- Fleet / Retail

---

## 5. Inspection & Estimate Creation

### 5.1 Technician Inspection

Technician performs:

- Requested service
- Multi-point inspection (recommended standard)

Findings must include:
- Condition
- Severity
- Recommendation

---

### 5.2 Building the Estimate

The estimate must be:

- **Accurate**
- **Itemized**
- **Transparent**

Include:

#### Labor
- Service description
- Time units (hours)

#### Parts
- Tires (size, brand, model)
- Components (brakes, filters, etc.)
- Quantity and unit price

#### Fees
- Shop supplies
- Disposal (e.g., tire disposal)

#### Taxes

---

### 5.3 Structuring the Estimate

Group work into:

- **Primary request items** (what customer asked for)
- **Recommended items** (inspection findings)
- **Deferred items** (non-urgent)

---

## 6. Customer Approval Process

### 6.1 Presenting the Estimate

Communicate clearly:

- What was found
- What is required vs optional
- Safety implications
- Cost breakdown

Avoid jargon. Use plain language.

---

### 6.2 Approval Methods

- Verbal (documented in system)
- Digital approval (preferred)
- Signed estimate

---

### 6.3 Approval Documentation

Record:

- Approved items
- Declined items
- Time/date
- Advisor initials

**Critical Rule:**
No work beyond initial authorization should proceed without customer approval.

---

### 6.4 Handling Objections

Common objections:
- Price
- Timing
- Trust

Recommended approach:
- Prioritize safety items
- Offer alternatives (good/better/best)
- Defer non-critical work with documentation

---

## 7. Work Execution

### 7.1 Dispatch

- Assign approved work to technician
- Ensure parts availability

---

### 7.2 Status Updates

For longer jobs:

- Notify customer of:
  - Delays
  - Additional findings
  - Completion estimate changes

---

### 7.3 Additional Work (Upsell Process)

If new issues are found:

1. Stop work (if necessary)
2. Build supplemental estimate
3. Contact customer
4. Obtain approval before proceeding

---

## 8. Job Completion & Quality Check

### 8.1 Technician Completion

- Mark work as complete
- Record notes and torque specs if applicable

---

### 8.2 Final Inspection

Advisor or manager verifies:

- Work completed matches approved estimate
- No outstanding issues
- Cleanliness (vehicle interior/exterior as applicable)

---

## 9. Checkout Process

### 9.1 Invoice Review

Ensure invoice matches:

- Approved estimate
- Any additional approved work

---

### 9.2 Customer Communication

Explain:

- Work performed
- Parts replaced
- Any deferred work
- Maintenance recommendations

---

### 9.3 Payment

Accept:

- Cash
- Card
- Fleet account
- Financing (if applicable)

---

### 9.4 Vehicle Return

- Provide keys
- Confirm customer satisfaction
- Thank the customer

---

## 10. Post-Service Follow-Up

### 10.1 Immediate

- Send receipt via email/SMS
- Provide warranty information

---

### 10.2 Short-Term (24–72 hours)

- Follow-up message:
  - “Is everything performing as expected?”

---

### 10.3 Long-Term

- Schedule next service reminder
- Add to marketing campaigns if applicable

---

## 11. Best Practices Summary

- Always document everything
- Never perform unapproved work
- Communicate early and often
- Prioritize safety and transparency
- Use structured estimates (clear grouping)
- Maintain consistent customer experience

---

## 12. Common Failure Points to Avoid

- Overbooking the schedule
- Incomplete vehicle information
- Vague estimates
- Performing work without approval
- Poor communication on delays
- Not documenting declined work

---

## 13. Key Metrics to Track

- Appointment utilization rate
- Estimate approval rate
- Average repair order (ARO)
- Technician efficiency
- Comebacks (rework rate)
- Customer satisfaction (NPS)

---

This guide provides a baseline operational model. It can be extended with digital workflows, integrations, and automation to improve throughput and customer experience.
